The letter, drawn as a letter

A first look at what replaces the post office's character-art envelope. Same rules as today: the outside is drawn from the envelope alone, three taps tear it open, and only then does the letter inside unfold. Comment on anything that feels off.

In a locker

    
The closed state is the letter's calling card in someone else's space. A locker's letters stand newest to oldest from left to right: the newest in front, here still sealed and asking to be opened soon, the older ones fanned out behind it. Hover one to lift it.

    

  


  

    

      
What it reads, and what it doesn't

      
    
        
  • Outside only, while closed. To, from, the postmark, the stamp, intention, need, stickers and your own envelope fields. Never a word of the inside: a sealed letter can't be read, so the drawing never needs it.
    • 
        
  • Stamps come as two presets or your own: your words (up to 30 characters) with a drawn picture or one you upload. The envelope already accepts any words as its stamp; only the picture is new.
    • 
        
  • Ink and stickers look made, not printed. The handwriting is blue-black ink with a slightly rough edge and uneven density, blended into the paper so its fibres show through. Every sticker gets a white die-cut border that follows the picture's own edge, transparent PNGs included.
    • 
        
  • The letter's own look is set inside the letter, in a ::: settings block at the top of its body (font: typewriter, later perhaps paper: lined). The shape is still declared in the YAML; the block only tunes it, and with the defaults there is no block at all. Because it sits inside, it is sealed with the letter. The greeting always comes from the envelope's to, so the renderer knows exactly where it ends.
    • 
        
  • Stickers are yours to place anywhere on the outside: upload a picture or try the sample, drag it, resize it. Here they live only in this page. Where they would be stored for a real letter (the post office can read the outside, and pictures are too big to write into the envelope itself) is a structure question, parked for later.
    • 
        
  • One date at a glance. The postmark carries the date once the post office has the letter. The written date joins it in pencil only when it is a different day, like a draft finished later or a date you chose, and stands alone before the letter is postmarked. It is always at the top of the letter paper too.
    • 
        
  • The first lines show wherever the key is. Everyone in the locker holds its key, so their browser unseals each opened letter and draws the paper peeking out with its first lines. The post office itself never has the key, so where it draws a letter (a link preview, the Your lockers list) the flap is torn and the paper blank. The haus's notes follow the same rule: first lines only where the note can be read.
    • 
        
  • Opened is the office's stamp. A letter someone opened shows its torn flap to everyone holding the link, the same fact the character art shows today with "opened".
    • 
        
  • Four pure functions, like the post office's closed.ts: renderClosed(envelope), renderTearing(envelope, tears), renderOpened(envelope, firstLines) and renderOpen(envelope, body). Data in, HTML out, so the post office, a haus and the visualizer catalog draw the same letter.
    • 
        
  • The light comes from the top right, like the haus's sun by day and its lamp at dusk. Paper and wax keep their own colors at night; only the light changes.
